Quality against ground truth

Why macro-F1 is the headline instead of accuracy

The answer key is mostly bug and enhancement. A model that falls back to bug whenever it's unsure will look fine on accuracy and be useless on the four small categories, which is exactly the failure that breaks triage. Macro-F1 gives every category equal weight, so skipping a small one costs the same as skipping a big one.

The excl. templated number leaves out the 26 bot-written CVE-…detected in… issues. A regex can spot those, so every model gets them right and the security score makes everyone look good. The gap between the two numbers is how much of that is going on.

Agreement

What agreement does and doesn't tell you

Agreement isn't accuracy. Both models can agree and both be wrong, and the big easy categories push the number up. What it's good for is sizing the review queue: the issues they disagree on are the ones a person should look at, so how many there are feeds straight into what production costs. Disagree on 20% here and 20% of the volume needs a second look.

Note that a person is the right destination, not a bigger model. Across the eleven models screened, every one scored between 0.44 and 0.67 on documentation, the hardest class. They fail on the same issues, so sending a disagreement to a larger model mostly buys another opinion of the same quality at a higher price.

Errors by type

Split up because each one needs a different fix. Rate limits mean drop the concurrency or ask for more quota. Timeouts mean raise the deadline or drop the model. Parse errors mean the model won't stick to the output format, which is a different problem from picking the wrong label.
