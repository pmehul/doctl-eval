Quality against ground truth

Why macro-F1 is the headline instead of accuracy

The answer key is mostly bug and enhancement. A model that falls back to bug whenever it's unsure will look fine on accuracy and be useless on the four small categories, which is exactly the failure that breaks triage. Macro-F1 gives every category equal weight, so skipping a small one costs the same as skipping a big one.

The excl. templated number leaves out the 26 bot-written CVE-…detected in… issues. A regex can spot those, so every model gets them right and the security score makes everyone look good. The gap between the two numbers is how much of that is going on.

Agreement

What agreement does and doesn't tell you

Agreement isn't accuracy. Both models can agree and both be wrong, and the big easy categories push the number up. What it's good for is sizing the review queue: the issues they disagree on are the ones a person should look at, so how many there are feeds straight into what production costs. Disagree on 20% here and 20% of the volume needs a second look.

Note that a person is the right destination, not a bigger model. Across the eleven models screened, every one scored between 0.44 and 0.67 on documentation, the hardest class. They fail on the same issues, so sending a disagreement to a larger model mostly buys another opinion of the same quality at a higher price.

Corpus and ground truth

How much a single run is worth

Less than you would think, and this is the most important thing on this page. A run is not reproducible even at temperature 0, because a hosted endpoint batches work across requests and that changes the arithmetic slightly.

same model, same 109 dev issues, six runs 0.789 · 0.810 · 0.810 · 0.774 · 0.793 · 0.789
mean / standard deviation / spread0.794 / 0.014 / 0.036
one configuration, measured twice0.847 and 0.774
first place to tenth on the screening table0.847 down to 0.777, a spread of 0.070

So one model's own noise is about half the distance between first and tenth place. Ranking eleven models by a single macro-F1 to three decimals was never valid, and when the two leaders were re-run five times each the order reversed: the model recommended here was third on that first table.

What follows from that. Quality claims come from five runs per model, not one. The recommendation rests on latency and cost, where the differences are two to three times and survive repetition, rather than on a macro-F1 gap that moves between runs. Where only a single run exists, it says so. make screen REPEAT=n runs each model n times and reports the spread, and it keeps the median run rather than the best, because picking the best of n is how a lucky draw becomes a headline.

Why macro-F1 and not accuracy

Two deliberately useless baselines, scored against the same answer key:

baselineaccuracymacro-F1
always answer bug47.8%0.108
answer bug unless the title looks like a CVE report54.9%0.280

Neither contains a model. Accuracy flatters both, because bug is 48% of the test split. Macro-F1 weights all six classes equally and catches them. It has the opposite weakness, though, and this run shows it: a class with 3 issues counts as much as one with 121, so a single flipped answer in a small class moves the headline. Both numbers are reported, and small classes are marked thin.

Candidate pool

Only DigitalOcean-hosted models, because that's where the exercise credits work. Picked to cover a wide range: size, whether the whole model runs per word or only a slice, whether it thinks out loud first, and about 20x in list price. On this workload, where a call is roughly 1,130 tokens in and 18 out, that works out at 18x between the cheapest and dearest model actually measured.
